# 5 Industries That Can Benefit From Professional Water Treatment Services

Water quality can have a significant impact on equipment, operational efficiency, product quality, and the overall performance of commercial facilities. Issues such as hard water, sediment, dissolved minerals, contaminants, and inconsistent water chemistry can contribute to scale buildup, corrosion, equipment damage, and unnecessary operating costs.

Professional water treatment services help businesses identify their unique water quality challenges and implement solutions designed around their facilities, equipment, and applications. While nearly any commercial operation can benefit from better water quality, the following five industries have particularly important water treatment needs.

## 1. Manufacturing

Manufacturing facilities use water for a wide variety of applications, including process water, rinsing, cleaning, cooling systems, boiler feed water, and equipment operation. Because water may play a direct role in production, inconsistent quality can affect both equipment performance and manufacturing processes.

Professional water treatment can help manufacturers address hardness, suspended solids, dissolved minerals, biofouling, and other water quality concerns. Depending on the application, manufacturers may use water softeners, filtration, reverse osmosis, deionization, UV treatment, or customized treatment systems.

Effective treatment can also help manufacturers pursue broader operational goals, such as reducing wastewater discharge, reusing water elsewhere in the facility, improving efficiency, and extending equipment life.

## 2. Healthcare

Healthcare facilities have complex water quality requirements, particularly in applications where water comes into contact with medical equipment or is used for critical processes.

Hospitals, surgical centers, clinics, and other healthcare organizations may require specialized treatment for sterile processing, boiler systems, cooling equipment, laboratories, and other applications. Healthcare facilities must also consider applicable water quality standards and regulatory requirements, including ANSI/AAMI ST108 requirements for water used in the processing of medical devices.

Professional water treatment providers can help healthcare organizations evaluate water quality, select appropriate treatment technologies, design systems, conduct testing, and develop ongoing maintenance strategies to support reliable performance and compliance.

## 3. Food and Beverage Processing

Water can directly influence the consistency, taste, appearance, and overall quality of food and beverage products. It is also used extensively for equipment cleaning, rinsing, steam generation, ingredient water, and other production processes.

Depending on the facility's incoming water and production requirements, treatment may be needed to address hardness, chlorine, sediment, microorganisms, dissolved minerals, and other water quality concerns.

Commercial filtration, water softening, reverse osmosis, UV treatment, and other technologies can provide manufacturers with more consistent water quality while helping protect processing equipment from scale and other water-related problems.

## 4. Hospitality and Lodging

Hotels and other lodging facilities rely on water throughout virtually every part of their operations. Guest rooms are only the beginning. Water is also critical for laundry, kitchens, dishwashing, boilers, hot water heaters, ice machines, coffee and beverage stations, swimming facilities, and cooling systems.

Hard water and other quality issues can contribute to spotting on glassware, poor laundry results, scale accumulation, inconsistent beverage quality, and premature wear on expensive equipment.

A properly designed commercial water treatment system can address these issues throughout the property. Professional maintenance and water testing can also help hospitality facilities maintain reliable performance as water usage and operating demands change.

## 5. Power Generation and Energy

Water quality plays a critical role in power generation and other energy applications, where boilers, steam systems, cooling towers, and other equipment may operate under demanding conditions.

Untreated or inadequately treated water can introduce minerals and contaminants that contribute to scale, corrosion, deposits, and declining heat-transfer efficiency. Over time, these issues can increase energy consumption, maintenance requirements, and the risk of equipment problems.

Technologies such as reverse osmosis, deionization, water softening, filtration, and boiler feed water treatment can help facilities achieve the water quality required by their systems. Ongoing testing and preventative maintenance can further help operators identify problems before they result in costly downtime.
